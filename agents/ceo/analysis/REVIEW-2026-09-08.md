## 1. Executive summary

**The product works, is unusually well-documented, and is not yet beautiful or provably smooth on the target hardware.** Built in four days by an agent team against an 83 KB PRD, the code is disciplined (TypeScript strict, byte-level contract tests, dt-independent camera, async GPU picking) and the pipeline is genuinely good. But the rendering layer has four verified defects that together mean the shipped app allocates roughly **300 MB of GPU render targets per second** at steady state, runs its canvas and post-processing at **1.5× pixel ratio even on 1× displays while drawing the scene at 1× and upscaling it**, has an **adaptive-quality ladder with no working rung**, and **never applies the filter dimming** the PRD specifies. None of this was visible on the M5 Pro Mac where every measurement was taken; all of it will matter on an Iris Xe laptop, which has never been tested.

### Verdicts

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Is three.js / WebGL2 the right base?** | **Yes.** The scene is ~10 draw calls of points, instanced quads and one card mesh. Its cost is memory bandwidth in full-screen passes, which no engine or API changes. WebGPU adds nothing measurable here and Firefox on Intel Macs still lacks it. Babylon, PlayCanvas and wasm engines are worse fits (§3.4). |
| **Is React + react-three-fiber the right way to drive it?** | **Not as used, and not necessary.** Two of the four verified rendering defects come from React state on the render path (a 500 ms `setInterval` re-renders the scene component; r3f then re-applies the `dpr` prop and the postprocessing wrapper rebuilds the bloom effect); the other two are library behaviour. Both React-side defects have one-line fixes inside r3f (§3.5). The case for taking React off the frame loop rests on owning the loop, label ordering and the post chain, not on these bugs alone. |
| **Is `@react-three/postprocessing` + `postprocessing` right?** | **No.** The bloom's `resolutionScale` is inert with `mipmapBlur`, the "selective" mask is inert, and the wrapper leaks effects. Own a ~600-line half-resolution bloom chain instead; it also removes ~65% of the frame's bandwidth (§3.3). |
| **Is the visual concept right?** | **It fights the data.** 57 of 87 planes are empty glows, only 2 planes can show spiral arms, 17% of cards are unstructured dust, and the cards' art — the one gorgeous asset — is invisible until the last level. Prototype a concept that shows art from the overview (§4). |
| **Code quality?** | High discipline, real bugs, heavy archaeology: 30% of non-blank lines are comments, ~5,500 lines of browser-driving review scripts (~4,650 of them one-off), and the dev harnesses, a 993-line GPU self-check and drei ship in the product bundle (§5, §6). |
| **Pipeline?** | Sound, deterministic, stdlib-only, well tested. A handful of dead fields, a name-keyed override table and layout laws that are inert on real data (§7). |

### The ten actions, in order

1. **Measure on the target.** One Iris Xe laptop, one 780M laptop, Chrome/Brave/Firefox, the protocol in §9. Everything below is prioritised on estimates until this exists.
2. **Stop the 2 Hz scene re-render** (`web/src/scene/EternitiesScene.tsx:487-503`) and construct the bloom effect once. This alone removes ~300 MB/s of GPU allocation churn (§2.2).
3. **Honour the pixel-ratio cap** — the `dpr` prop at `EternitiesScene.tsx:824` is re-applied by r3f on every render and defeats the ladder's only real rung (§3.2); the one-line fix is `dpr={[0.5, tier.tier.pixelRatioCap]}`.
4. **Upload the filter mask** (`web/src/scene/starfield/starGeometry.ts:180-194` has no production caller); PRD 5.8 dimming is absent.
5. **Replace the post chain** with an owned half-res bloom (§3.5); delete `@react-three/postprocessing` and `postprocessing`.
6. **Delete the dead 491 KB shard fetch** on every page load (`web/src/scene/useSceneData.ts:154-165`) — together with, or after, retiring `verify-browser.mjs`, which waits for the log line this fetch produces (§6.1).
7. **Take the harnesses, bench runner, self-check and drei out of the production bundle** (`web/src/App.tsx:55-59` static imports).
8. **Fix the quality monitor thresholds** (`web/src/scene/quality/adaptiveQuality.ts:65-72`): it cannot restore on 60 Hz and accepts 50–59 fps.
9. **Decide the visual concept with a three-day prototype** of "worlds" (§4.4) on the Iris Xe laptop before any further tuning of galaxies.
10. **Then de-React the render loop and modernise** (three r185, own frame loop; §3.6), and run the simplification checklist (§10).

### 2.2 The allocation churn, explained

In every 6-second phase the tour recorded 12 identical allocation bursts, i.e. two per second, each containing: two 2880×1620 RGBA16F targets, one 2880×1620 RGBA8 + depth pair, one 1920×1080 RGBA16F + depth pair, and a full 8-level mip chain from 1440×810 down to 12×7 — about 150 MB of GPU memory per burst. That is one `SelectiveBloomEffect` being constructed from scratch, twice a second, for the whole session. The 1920×1080 pair is the composer's own scene buffer: `EffectComposer.setSize` sizes it once, at mount, from the drawing buffer as it was then — dpr 1.0 on this 1× viewport, because `StarScene`'s mount effect had just applied `min(1.5, devicePixelRatio)` (`StarScene.tsx:257-266`) — and `createDepthTexture` disposes and reallocates it at that size on every pass swap (`node_modules/postprocessing/build/index.js:912-938`), while the new effect's targets are sized by `addPass` at the *current* drawing buffer, 2880×1620 (`index.js:998-1005`). So on a 1× display the scene is drawn at 1× and upscaled through a 1.5× post chain into a 1.5× canvas: blurrier and dearer at once. The chain of cause is fully verified in the sources (postprocessing 6.36.6, @react-three/postprocessing 2.19.1, r3f 8.18.0, three 0.170.0):

1. `EternitiesScene.tsx:487-503` — a 500 ms `setInterval` calls `setGpu(handle.gpuBytes)` and `setThumbnails({...})`; both are fresh objects every tick (`web/src/scene/cards/CardTier.tsx:183-185` returns a new object from the `gpuBytes` getter), so React re-renders `SceneView`.
2. r3f's `<Canvas>` pushes its children into its own reconciler in a layout effect with no dependency array (`web/node_modules/@react-three/fiber/dist/react-three-fiber.esm.js:63-102`), so `Effects` (`web/src/scene/Effects.tsx:109-148`) re-renders.
3. The `SelectiveBloom` wrapper lists its `...props` rest object in its `useMemo` deps (`web/node_modules/@react-three/postprocessing/dist/effects/SelectiveBloom.js`, deps end in `props`), so a new `SelectiveBloomEffect` is constructed on every render.
4. `EffectComposer`'s pass list is keyed on `children`; it removes and re-adds the `EffectPass`, and `removePass` does not dispose (`node_modules/postprocessing/build/index.js:1039`). The old effect and its ~20 render targets are dropped for the garbage collector.

The Mac absorbs this. A Windows laptop with shared memory and a D3D11 driver, where each allocation is a resource creation, will not.

### 2.3 What it looks like

Eight captures were taken (home, Dominaria, Theros, Rabiah, card sheet + focus, flipped DFC, Blind Eternities, attract). Judged as a viewer, not a tester:

- **Home** reads as a competent starfield with 82 labels crowding the centre. The 57 empty planes are pale grey smudges that outnumber the real galaxies two to one; several sit in the foreground as large blurred ellipses.
- **Dominaria (6,266 cards)** is a coloured cloud with a bright central bar. No spiral arm is legible at framing distance, even after the DEC-684 arm-width re-cut. That is a claim about perceived legibility, not a dispute of the accepted gate: the DEC-683/684 evidence measured a geometric lane between arm centrelines (the repo keeps the invariant as `pipeline/tests/test_pipeline_invariants.py:404-421`, a ≥ 15° lane at balanced colour) and the owner judged captures at true CSS scale. Both can hold at once: a 21.6° lane exists in the angular distribution and disappears on screen under ±0.35-band radial jitter, a dense bulge, 36 overlapping bands and 6 px bloomed sprites. The gate measures geometry; it does not measure what a viewer sees (T7, §5.5). Neighbouring empty planes render as big grey out-of-focus blobs in front of it; they are the most visually damaging element in every plane-level capture.
- **Theros (783)** is an elongated diffuse cloud; **Rabiah (75)** is a scatter of dots inside a grey haze with a single band label.
- **Card level** is the pile the Phase 3 captures already showed: thumbnails at random depth and orientation overlapping around the focused card. The focused card itself, its tilt, and the flip are good.
- **Blind Eternities** is bokeh: soft discs of colour in haze.
- **Attract mode** works; the HUD hides and the camera drifts.

"Smooth" holds on this machine. "Beautiful" does not, and the reasons are structural (§4), not tuning.

## 3. Tech-stack verdict

### 3.1 Three layers, three different answers

**three.js on WebGL2 — keep.** The heavy lifting is already engine-agnostic: one `Points` of 28,587 stars with a custom GLSL ES 3.0 shader (`web/src/scene/starfield/starFieldObjects.ts:130-158`), all motion in the vertex shader from a 6×87 RGBA32F table read with `texelFetch` (`web/src/scene/starfield/shaders.ts:196-217`), an instanced glow quad (`starFieldObjects.ts:216-260`), an instanced thumbnail quad (`web/src/scene/cards/thumbnailTier.ts`), a card mesh, and an 11×11 id-buffer pick target read back asynchronously through a fence (`web/src/scene/picking/idPicker.ts:91-100`, `:181-200`). three supplies matrices, GL state, render targets and `readRenderTargetPixelsAsync`, for 182 KB as served. The star field costs well under 1 ms of GPU on this Mac and, by extrapolation, on the floor (an estimate until §9 runs). Nothing in this scene needs compute shaders, storage buffers or bindless textures. **The frame's cost is memory bandwidth in full-screen passes, and that is the same on WebGL2, WebGPU, Babylon or a custom renderer.**

**React + react-three-fiber — remove from the render path.** r3f contributes a canvas, a resize path and a frame loop; every GPU object is built imperatively and mounted via `<primitive>`. In exchange it places React renders on the frame path: the component that owns `<Canvas>` and `<Effects>` holds eight pieces of state (`EternitiesScene.tsx:230-245`) and is re-rendered at 2 Hz by its own interval; r3f then re-runs `configure()` on every render and re-applies the `dpr` prop (`node_modules/@react-three/fiber/dist/events-776716bd.esm.js:2013`: `if (dpr && state.viewport.dpr !== calculateDpr(dpr)) state.setDpr(dpr)`). There are also three separate `requestAnimationFrame` loops per frame (r3f, `web/src/labels/PlaneLabels.tsx:207-215`, and the planet hover label at `EternitiesScene.tsx:136-160`) with no ordering guarantee against the camera update. The team documented the dpr race at length (`EternitiesScene.tsx:810-824`) and concluded the prop was "inert"; it was measured only under `?quality=` pins, where the prop and the pin agree. Under the free ladder the prop wins twice a second. Both React-side defects are cheap to fix in place (§3.5); what r3f cannot give back is ownership of the frame order.

**`@react-three/postprocessing` + `postprocessing` — replace.** Three verified defects, each in the library, none in the app's intent:

| Defect | Where | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `resolutionScale` ignored when `mipmapBlur` is on | `node_modules/postprocessing/build/index.js:3896-3899` — `BloomEffect.setSize` sizes `luminancePass` and `mipmapBlurPass` at full `width, height`; the scaled target feeds only the unused Kawase path | the quality ladder's bloom rung (`adaptiveQuality.ts:34`) changes nothing; the `?probe` check reads the unused target (`EternitiesScene.tsx:554`) |
| Selection is inert | `index.js:9261` (`depthMode = EqualDepth`) and `:5941` (`KEEP_MAX_DEPTH`): keep = scene depth is 1.0 **or** equals the selection's depth | the field blooms because it writes no depth; cards are excluded because they do. The selection `DepthPass` (28.6k points through `MeshDepthMaterial`, applied to `Points` by three r170 at `three.module.js:30456`) and the full-res mask pass are pure cost |
| Effect rebuilt per render, never disposed | `SelectiveBloom.js` `useMemo` deps include `props`; `removePass` at `index.js:1039` does not dispose | §2.2. Fixed upstream: @react-three/postprocessing 3.1.1 keys the memo on scalars (needs r3f 9 / React 19); postprocessing 6.39.4 keeps the two behaviours above |

The app's `Effects.tsx:42-66` records the second pass as a known cost and attributes the card exclusion to the selection; the mechanism is actually `depthWrite: true` on the card tier versus `false` on the field (`starFieldObjects.ts:138`, `:247`). If someone ever set `depthWrite: false` on the card, it would bloom.

### 3.2 The quality ladder has no working rung

| Rung | Intended | Actual in the shipped app |
|---|---|---|
| 0→1 pixel ratio 1.5→1.0 | real | **defeated**: `dpr={QUALITY_TIERS[pinnedTier ?? 0].pixelRatioCap}` (`EternitiesScene.tsx:824`) is 1.5 whenever nothing is pinned; r3f re-applies it on every render. Measured: canvas stayed 2880×1620 through `full → pixel-ratio → bloom` on a dpr-1 viewport while the scene buffer stayed 1920×1080 |
| 1→2 bloom resolution 0.5→0.25 | real | **inert** in postprocessing (above) |
| 2→3 thumbnail capacity 512→256 | frees GPU memory | reduces per-frame instance writes only; the 64 MB atlas is a fixed allocation (`web/src/scene/cards/atlas.ts:57`) |
| monitor | degrade at p90 > 20 ms, restore at p90 < 13.5 ms (`adaptiveQuality.ts:65-72`) | on a 60 Hz panel a healthy frame reports 16.7 ms > 13.5, so it can never restore; the 20 ms trigger is PRD 7.2's own 50 fps ceiling, so 50–59 fps is accepted as fine — the owner's 60 fps floor is stricter than the PRD |

Consequences for the target: a 1080p Windows laptop at 100% scaling draws the scene at 1920×1080 but runs the post chain and canvas at 2880×1620 — 2.25× the post-processing pixels for an upscaled, blurrier picture; at 125% the same mismatch (scene 2400×1350); at 150% everything is consistently 2880×1620; a 1440p laptop at 150% renders 3840×2160, i.e. 4K, everywhere, on an integrated GPU, with no way down.

### 3.3 GPU cost on an Iris Xe — an estimate, with assumptions

This is arithmetic, not a measurement, and §9 exists to replace it. Assumptions: ~1 TFLOP FP32, ~50 GB/s shared LPDDR4x of which 40–50 GB/s is reachable; immediate-mode rasteriser, so every blended fragment is a read-modify-write of the RGBA16F target (16 B) and every full-screen pass streams inputs and outputs; texture taps ~4 B effective; blur kernels ~5× write bytes after cache. A 1080p laptop today draws the scene at 1920×1080 (2.07 Mpx) and everything after it at 2880×1620 (4.67 Mpx) (§3.2); a 1440p laptop at 150% draws 3840×2160 (8.29 Mpx) throughout. The focused plane's glow quad spans 4.2 plane radii (`shaders.ts:381`, `web/src/scene/tuning.ts:112`) while the plane tether frames at 3.2 radii (`web/src/camera/framing.ts:104-114`), so at plane level it covers most of the screen with a two-tap noise + integer-hash fragment shader (`shaders.ts:438-451`). `BloomEffect` carries no `CONVOLUTION` attribute in 6.36, so the wrapper merges bloom and vignette into one composite pass.

| Pass (plane level) | 1080p laptop today (scene 2.07 Mpx, post 4.67 Mpx) | 1440p at 150% (8.29 Mpx throughout) |
|---|---|---|
| Scene clear, RGBA16F + depth | 25 MB | 100 MB |
| Background + stars, additive | 2 MB | 8 MB |
| Glow overdraw (~0.6 screen × 24 B) | 32 MB | 132 MB |
| Selection depth pass (inert, §3.1) | 37 MB | 66 MB |
| Depth-mask pass (2 depth + colour in, 16F out) | 112 MB | 199 MB |
| Luminance pass at full res (inert scale, §3.1) | 75 MB | 133 MB |
| Mip chain from full res | 123 MB | 219 MB |
| Bloom + vignette composite (one merged pass) | 50 MB | 116 MB |
| Browser compositor | 37 MB | 66 MB |
| **Total per frame** | **~490 MB → 10–12 ms** | **~1,040 MB → 21–26 ms** |

Card level adds thumbnail fill, planets and a larger glow: **~12 / ~24 ms**. ALU is not the limit; the post chain is 65–70% of the bytes and glow overdraw is second. The star field is a rounding error. The 40–50 GB/s assumption fits a DDR4 Iris Xe; LPDDR5 parts and the Radeon 780M (~90 GB/s effective with compression) have materially more headroom, so read these as the pessimistic end of the floor.

**Reading:** at 1080p, 10–12 ms of GPU time plus the 2 Hz allocation bursts is *marginal*; expect dropped frames at plane and card level, and a picture that is a 1× render upscaled through the post chain. At 1440p at 150%, 60 fps is **not plausible today**, and the ladder cannot rescue it. After the redesign in §3.5 (bloom source drawn at half res, no depth/mask/luminance passes, one honest and consistent pixel ratio): 1080p at dpr 1.0 ≈ 135 MB → ~3 ms; 1440p at dpr 1.5 ≈ 465 MB → ~10 ms, or ≈ 210 MB → ~4.5 ms at dpr 1.0. 60 fps becomes plausible everywhere in scope, with dpr 1.0 as a real fallback rather than a hope.

### 3.5 Recommendation and target architecture

**First, regardless, in an afternoon:** `dpr={[0.5, tier.tier.pixelRatioCap]}` at `EternitiesScene.tsx:824` — r3f clamps `devicePixelRatio` into the range (`node_modules/@react-three/fiber/dist/events-776716bd.esm.js:421-426`), so `configure()` re-applies the same value and the ladder's first rung becomes real; hoist the 2 Hz stats out of the `<Canvas>` owner into a ref polled by a HUD leaf; give `Effects` stable props (or move to the 3.x wrapper, which needs r3f 9 / React 19). That removes R1 and R2.

**Then, the recommendation: keep three.js on WebGL2 and upgrade to r185. Remove `@react-three/fiber`, `@react-three/drei`, `@react-three/postprocessing` and `postprocessing`. Own the frame loop and the post chain in ~600 lines of imperative TypeScript. React renders the HUD, overlays and a `<canvas>` host, nothing else.** This is a boundary change, not an engine change, and it is justified by frame-loop ownership, label ordering and the post-chain rewrite — not by the two bugs above, which the afternoon fix already closes.

- **Frame loop owner.** One `SceneRenderer` created in `createServices()` next to the navigation host and router (`web/src/main.tsx:27-30`), owning the canvas, the `WebGLRenderer`, one `requestAnimationFrame`, and a fixed order per tick: input → `rig.update(dt)` → plane table → uniforms → async pick → main pass → bloom source → mip chain → composite → label tick → quality sample. `PlaneLabels` and the planet label become frame subscribers called after the camera matrices are final, so label lag is zero by construction.
- **React attachment.** Inbound is imperative calls driven by store subscriptions: `focusStar`, `setFilterMask`, `setReducedMotion`, `setQualityCap`, `setLabelsEnabled`. Outbound is a mutable `FrameStats` snapshot polled by one HUD leaf, so a 2 Hz poll re-renders a `<li>`, not the renderer. Lint rule: no module under `ui/` or `app/` imports `three`. Bench, probe and self-check drive the same class from a separate `harness.html` entry and leave the product bundle.
- **Bloom.** The bloom source is not the scene buffer. Stars, glow and background are drawn a second time with a `BLOOM_PASS` define (threshold and knee in the fragment shaders, the same `dim` term so filter dimming still drops a star out of bloom) into a **half-resolution** RGBA16F target; cards, thumbnails and planets are never drawn into it, which is the correct and free "selective". A 5–6 level 13-tap down / 9-tap up chain runs at ≤ half res. One composite pass reads scene + bloom, applies vignette, a filmic roll-off (hot cores stop clipping to white — the cheapest beauty win available) and sRGB encoding. No depth pass, no mask, no luminance pass, no per-render reconstruction.
- **Quality tiers that do something.** 0→1 dpr cap 1.5→1.0 (honoured, because nothing re-applies a prop); 2: bloom source ½→¼ and 6→5 levels; 3: thumbnail capacity; new 4: a cheap glow variant (one tap, no dither) because glow overdraw is the second cost. The monitor measures the display's refresh (median rAF interval over 60 frames) and defines "fine" as p90 ≤ one vsync + 0.5 ms; degrade at p90 > 1.05 vsync over 1.5 s. Chromium's `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` gives per-pass GPU time for the bench.
- **Card level.** One instanced sphere draw and a `TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` of 256×187 art crops replaces 72 meshes + 72 pick meshes each with their own `ShaderMaterial` (`web/src/scene/cards/focusedCard.ts:496-529`): 4 main draws and 3 pick draws instead of up to 155. This matters most on Firefox, whose out-of-process WebGL makes every call an IPC.
- **DPR and monitors.** `ResizeObserver` with `device-pixel-content-box` sizes the backing store exactly; a re-armed `matchMedia('(resolution: Xdppx)')` listener catches window moves between monitors. Cap = `min(tier.cap, devicePixelRatio)`, applied by the renderer, not by a prop.
- **Shader compilation.** The scene links 13 programs in total (15 on Firefox), measured in the tours — the 72 planet materials share one program through three's parameter-keyed cache (`three.module.js:21192`), so R6 is a draw-call problem, not a compile problem. Each program is linked on first draw and three's `onFirstUse` then calls `getProgramInfoLog` synchronously; DEC-645 measured a 362 ms and a 322 ms stall from exactly this on Metal, and `checkShaderErrors = false` only moves the stall. On Windows every browser compiles through ANGLE's D3D11/FXC path, which is markedly slower than Metal, so the first plane focus and the first card focus are the likely hitches. Link every program at boot behind `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` (three r170 already polls `COMPLETION_STATUS_KHR` in `WebGLProgram.isReady`, `three.module.js:20444-20460`, and exposes `renderer.compileAsync`, `:29895`) and warm them during the 4 s intro; never link on first draw.
- **Feature detection (`capabilities.ts`, at boot).** WebGL2; `EXT_color_buffer_float` (else RGBA8 targets with the existing dither, tiers capped at 2); `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE ≥ 4096`; `ALIASED_POINT_SIZE_RANGE` (clamp `uMaxPixels` and the pick floor to it, log it into bench JSON); `MAX_ARRAY_TEXTURE_LAYERS ≥ 72`; a 1 ms half-float probe (draw four `HALF_FLOAT` points at known positions into a 16×16 target and read back) that selects float32 automatically instead of the manual `?positions=` switch (`web/src/scene/starfield/starGeometry.ts:50-67`). Repack the star record client-side into a 16-byte aligned buffer (position 3×half + pad, class 3×u8, style 3×u8, filter u8, thumb u8) so ANGLE/D3D11 does not repack the current 12-byte record with byte attributes at offsets 6 and 9 (`starGeometry.ts:117-122`); the data contract does not change. Never branch on `RENDERER` strings — Brave farbles them.

## 4. Visual concept

### 4.1 The metaphor versus the data

The PRD's promise (`prd_v3.md:219`) is that "a galaxy's shape from afar is literally its cards' positions". The shipped dataset (`web/public/data/6d4779695fde33ea/planes.json`, `pipeline/reports/2026-09-06.md:90-180`) makes that promise unkeepable:

| Fact | Number | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Planes with zero cards | **57 of 87** | rendered as small dim glows (`tuning.ts:120-123`) that PRD 5.3.8 obliges the app to label; the home view is 82 labels over 30 real objects |
| Planes that can show spiral arms | **2** (Dominaria 6,266; Ravnica 2,286) | criterion 2 of PRD 9.3 had to be raised from 200 to 2,000 cards (`prd_v3.md:622`) because "arm-width tuning cannot add stars" |
| Cards in the Blind Eternities | **4,980 = 17.4%** | the largest population after Dominaria renders as unstructured dust with no arms, bands or spin |
| Dominaria's share of everything | **22%** | its core is dense enough that 23 of 24 self-check samples are occluded on a clean build (`docs/star-renderer.md:243-247`) |
| Colour-skew arm law | inert | Magic's colour pie is balanced; `arm_width_scale` lands 0.98–1.03 on every plane over 500 cards (`pipeline/src/eternities/fixtures/layout.py:343-347`) |
| Gold-heavy planes | Alara 58%, Ravnica 39% multicolour | the bulge (`r *= BULGE_SCALE`, 0.3, `layout.py:37,213`) swallows the arms |
| Single-set planes | 5 | the chronology radius is a constant; on multi-band planes the innermost band's law puts its stars on the 0.02 inner clamp (measured from `stars.bin`: 114 of Dominaria's stars, 1.8%, and 2.8% of Ravnica's sit exactly on the clamp ring) |
| Art visible at overview | none | the 12-byte record carries hue class, not colour from the image (`docs/data-contract.md:136-149`) |

And the star rendering itself: a soft disc up to 22 px with a squared-smoothstep falloff (`shaders.ts:342-343`) under a 0.28-threshold, 1.15-intensity bloom (`tuning.ts:133-135`) reads as out-of-focus bokeh, not as stars. The card sheet is a pile by construction: thumbnails are 0.30 world units tall (`tuning.ts:167`) in a disc where Dominaria's mean star spacing is ~0.24 units, viewed from 2.2 units.

### 4.2 Three candidate concepts

**A — Galaxy, fixed.** Keep astrophotography; replace the laws the data cannot support. Empty planes collapse into one faint outer halo with a single label ("57 uncharted planes"), names on hover. Radius ∝ N^0.4 instead of log N (`layout.py:77-83`) so Dominaria's radius is 3.3× Mercadia's instead of 1.3× (its card count is 20×). Two-arm grand-design spirals (M51, not a five-arm pinwheel) with colour running *along* the arm and equal-area chronology bands. Dust becomes a cosmic web, one filament per set. **The key fix for the sheet: "constellation resolve"** — as the camera crosses the 14–24 px band (`tuning.ts:158-159`), stars relax from true positions into a non-overlapping hex sheet on the disc plane, driven by camera distance (a per-plane relaxed-position buffer computed in the existing worker). Stars become 1–3 px cores with a Gaussian PSF and spikes only on the brightest 2%. WebGL2, cost class B on Iris Xe. **Limit:** 26 of 28 spirals still have too few stars for arms, and the art remains a reward for the last level.

**B — Worlds (recommended for prototyping).** Planes are worlds, which is what they are in the lore. Each plane is a globe tiled with its cards' art; radius ∝ √N so every card owns a cell of constant size (Dominaria ≈ 45 cells across, Shenmeng ≈ 3: a giant and a moon, the 22% share *visible*). The 57 empty planes are dark moons in the outer system, unlit and unlabelled until hover. On the globe, **latitude = colour** (five bands, gold as an equatorial belt, colourless as ice caps) and **longitude = time** (each set a meridian slice) — Alara wears a golden belt, New Phyrexia has ice caps, a single-set plane is one slice and looks complete. From framing distance a world is a mosaic that reads as terrain; the camera dollies in and the tether slides from the centre to the surface point under the reticle; cells above ~24 px fade from swatch to art. Click plucks the card off the surface (tilt and flip as today); printings orbit it as a **flat ring of `small` cards**, not spheres. The Blind Eternities is a belt around the whole system, one arc per set. Rendering: one sphere per world with a baked 256×128 cell-colour texture (~4 MB for all worlds) at distance, instanced opaque quads sampling a `TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` near; no full-scene bloom, rim glow in the atmosphere shader. Cost class A far, B near (~2,000 opaque textured quads ≈ 2–3 ms). WebGL2 suffices. **The enabler:** the pipeline fetches each card's `small` image once (28,587 × ~10 KB; the image CDN has no rate limit, `docs/scryfall-policy.md:80`) and bakes a 2×2 RGB565 swatch, 8 bytes per card (~230 KB `swatches.bin`) — a colour statistic, not a mirrored image. Live art: `art_crop` letterboxed into 128×96 layers, 1,024 layers ≈ 50 MB (67 MB with mips), about today's 64 MB atlas.

**C — Strata.** Time is the data's strongest structure: a streamgraph of card art, 1993→2026 on the horizontal axis, each plane a stratum whose thickness is its card count per year, the Blind Eternities as grey bedrock underneath, empty planes absent and indexed. Continuous deep zoom to art; the card lifts off and its printings fan beneath it at their actual release years. One quadtree of instanced tiles, class A, a map camera (pan/zoom with inertia). It deliberately breaks PRD 5.1.1 ("photoreal space, not data visualisation"). **Its one-hour still — a streamgraph rendered from `planes.json` set counts — is worth making regardless.**

| | A Galaxy, fixed | B Worlds | C Strata |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art visible from the overview | no | yes (mosaic) | yes (mosaic) |
| Giant plane | bigger galaxy, occluded core | honest size, no occlusion | thick stratum |
| 57 empties | one halo | dark moons | absent, indexed |
| 17% dust | set filaments | set arcs on a belt | bedrock by set |
| Tiny planes | still too few stars | small moon, 30 big tiles | hairline lens |
| Truly 3D / continuous zoom | yes / yes (with relax) | yes / yes | 2.5D / yes |
| Iris Xe cost class (estimate) | B–C | A / B | A |
| Contract churn | low | low + swatches + artist field | medium + swatches |
| Fits PRD 5.1 | yes | mostly | no |

### 4.3 Recommendation

**Prototype B in three days, on the Iris Xe laptop, before any further galaxy tuning.** Dominaria and Rabiah as instanced cells on spheres with fake swatches (hue colour + noise), art streaming for near cells, the surface-following tether. If the mosaic-to-art transition and the tether feel right there, build it. If not, A's "constellation resolve" is the single change that fixes the worst current failure (the pile) inside the existing renderer, and C's still costs an hour. Independent of the choice, three rules follow from the captures: never draw 57 grey blobs; show colour derived from the cards at the overview; make stars (if any) small and sharp with a real PSF rather than bokeh discs.

### 4.4 Compliance notes (Scryfall terms, fetched 2026-09-08)

- "Do not distort, skew, or stretch card images" and "Do not blur, sharpen, desaturate, or color-shift card images". The planet shader wraps `art_crop` onto spheres with equirect UVs and applies wrapped-lambert shading (0.35–1.15) plus a rim (`web/src/scene/cards/cardShaders.ts:217-265`). That is a geometric distortion and a brightness shift of the art. The repo's own policy note checked only attribution for `art_crop` (`docs/scryfall-policy.md:113`). Flat printings (B) remove the question.
- "When using the `art_crop`, list the artist name and copyright elsewhere in the same interface". The printing tuple carries no artist field (`docs/data-contract.md:283`); today the full card is shown alongside, which satisfies the alternative clause. Any concept that shows art crops without the full card needs the artist in the contract.
- Bulk fetching for swatches is not forbidden ("you may not simply repackage, republish, or proxy Scryfall data"); an 8-byte colour statistic is none of those. Fetch once, politely, cache by `imageTs`.
